How the poll works

One question: on a neutral field, who beats whom? Every team gets one number, its power rating: how many points better it is than an average team (an average FBS team in college, an average team in the NFL). The ranking is just those numbers sorted.

Because it's one number per team, the order holds all the way down. #1 is favored over every team in the poll, #2 over everyone below it, and so on. The gap between two teams is the neutral-field point spread. If #1 is 27.2 and #9 is 20.9, #1 is a 6.3-point favorite.

What goes in

1 · The betting market (2/3 of the weight). Every finished game's closing spread, plus the current spread on every game this week. A spread tells you how far apart the books think two teams are, after injuries, quarterback play and the eye test are already priced in. Chain hundreds of spreads together across a season and each team ends up with a consistent number.

2 · Results on the field (1/3). The actual final margin of every game, capped at 28 points in college and 21 in the NFL, so running it up on a bad team doesn't count. This is the résumé part: you have to win, and close wins against good teams count more than blowouts of bad ones.

How it's fit

Every game becomes one equation: rating(home) − rating(away) + home field ≈ spread or margin. The poll finds the set of ratings that fits all of them at once (weighted least squares), so strength of schedule is built in. Beating a team only helps as much as that team is good.

Recent games count more: market lines fade with a 5-week half-life, results with 8. Home-field advantage is fitted from the data, not assumed. A light prior keeps teams with very few games from swinging wildly (FCS opponents start around −18).

Reading a team

Books = the rating from betting lines alone. On-field = the rating from capped results alone. When those two disagree, the market and the scoreboard see a team differently. That's where to look for overrated and underrated teams. vs #1 = its chance to beat the top team on a neutral field.

Updates

It rebuilds itself from live ESPN scores and DraftKings lines at most every 30 minutes. New lines for next week's games move it during the week, and final scores move it on game day. Arrows compare against last week's final poll.
